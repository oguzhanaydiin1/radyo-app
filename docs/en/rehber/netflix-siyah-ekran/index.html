Sound but no picture on Netflix: the black screen problem

The episode starts, you hear the sound, but the screen stays black. This usually isn’t a fault: it means the copy protection (DRM) thinks something is recording.

Why does it happen?

Services such as Netflix, Disney+, Prime Video and TV+ stream with copy protection (DRM) to stop videos from being copied. If the browser or computer suspects the screen could be recorded, the picture is shown black while the sound keeps playing. Common causes:

  • A screen recording or screen share that’s running (OBS, a Discord stream, Teams/Zoom screen sharing, some screenshot tools).
  • Graphics acceleration turned off in the browser, or an outdated graphics driver.
  • A monitor, cable or adapter that doesn’t support copy protection (HDCP), especially on a second screen.
  • Some apps and browsers that embed the video inside another web view (browser windows inside desktop apps).

Try these in order

  1. Stop screen recording and sharing. If OBS, Discord, Teams or similar is streaming or sharing, stop it and reload the page.
  2. Turn on graphics acceleration. In Chrome, Settings → System → Use graphics acceleration when available; in Edge, the same option under Settings → System and performance. Then restart the browser.
  3. Update your graphics driver from NVIDIA, AMD or Intel’s own app, or from Windows Update.
  4. Check the monitor. Try the video on your main screen; unplug the second screen or adapter for a moment.
  5. Open it another way. The service’s Windows app or another browser shows where the problem is.

In Pusula Browser

The Windows version of Pusula recognises copy-protected streams (Netflix, Disney+, Prime Video, TV+, Exxen and similar) and plays the video in a separate layer inside the tab so the picture shows; you don’t need to do anything. If the picture still stays black on a site that isn’t on the list, choose Fix video not showing from the menu.
