Blocking ads on your phone (Android)

On your phone, ads cover the screen and eat into your data plan. Without installing anything, or with a few minutes of setup, you can cut them down a lot.

1. Use a browser that blocks ads

Most default phone browsers (Chrome included) don’t block ads, and Chrome on Android doesn’t allow extensions. Switching to an ad-blocking browser is the easiest way:

  • Pusula Browser: ad and tracker blocking is built in and on by default, and it declines cookie pop-ups for you. The address bar sits at the bottom of the screen, within reach of your thumb.
  • Firefox: supports extensions on Android; install uBlock Origin to block ads.
  • Samsung Internet: comes with Samsung phones; ad-blocking add-ons can be added in its settings.

2. For the whole phone: Private DNS

On Android 9 and later, entering a “Private DNS” address that blocks ad servers can also block some ads inside apps:

  1. Go to Settings → Connections (or Network & internet) → Private DNS. The menu name can vary slightly by phone.
  2. Choose Private DNS provider hostname and enter the address of an ad-blocking DNS service (for example AdGuard DNS: dns.adguard-dns.com).
  3. Save. If a site or app has problems, switch back to Automatic in the same place.

Careful: with Private DNS, the domain names you visit go to that DNS service. Choose one you trust, with a clear privacy policy.

Installing Pusula on Android

  1. On your phone, tap Download for Android below.
  2. Open the downloaded file. If the phone asks, turn on “Allow from this source”, then tap Install.
  3. If you like, choose Pusula in Settings → Apps → Default apps → Browser app so links open in Pusula.

If you also use Pusula on your computer, sign in with the same OA Labs account to sync bookmarks and open tabs between the two.

Pusula Browser is free, for Windows and Android.
